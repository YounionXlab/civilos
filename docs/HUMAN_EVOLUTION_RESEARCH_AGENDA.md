## Purpose

This document turns the Human Evolution Key Questions list into a structured research agenda for X-LAB.

The agenda is not a list of predictions or conclusions. It is a registry of important questions that may be explored through research, modeling, simulation, experiments, narrative worlds, interactive experiences, and real-world decision systems.

The canonical flow is:

```text
Key Question
    ↓
Research Track
    ↓
Experiment / Model / Scenario
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Insight
    ↓
Real-world Decision
    ↓
Feedback
```

Questions use the canonical identifier format:

```text
Q-001
Q-002
Q-003
...
```

Experiments may then reference one or more questions using identifiers such as:

```text
EXP-CIV-001
EXP-CLIMATE-001
EXP-BIO-001
EXP-MAT-001
EXP-SOC-001
EXP-SPACE-001
```

## Research Domains

The current agenda is organized into seven broad domains.

1. Human Capability and Cognition
2. Society and Institutions
3. Health, Life, and Biological Evolution
4. Climate, Ecology, and Planetary Resilience
5. Energy, Materials, and Industrial Capability
6. Planetary and Multi-body Civilization
7. Long-term Civilization and Cosmic Expansion

These domains are research tracks, not product silos. A single question may belong to more than one track.

---

# 1. Human Capability and Cognition

## Q-001 — Human-AI Capability Boundary

How should humans divide work, judgment, learning, and agency with increasingly capable AI systems so that human capability grows rather than atrophies?

Possible research directions:

- human-AI division of labor;
- cognitive offloading;
- judgment quality;
- learning acceleration;
- capability circles;
- human autonomy.

## Q-002 — Human Learning in the AI Era

What forms of education best prepare humans for a world in which information access and machine intelligence are abundant?

## Q-003 — Wisdom and Decision Quality

How can humans improve long-term judgment under uncertainty, complexity, and information overload?

## Q-004 — Human Self-regulation and State

How can people reliably improve physical, emotional, cognitive, and attentional state without depending entirely on external intervention?

---

# 2. Society and Institutions

## Q-005 — War and Large-scale Conflict

Can advanced societies reduce war, coercion, exploitation, and destructive zero-sum competition without creating stagnation or extreme centralization?

Candidate experiment:

```text
EXP-CIV-003 / Zero War
```

## Q-006 — Governance under Complexity

What governance systems remain adaptive when technology, population, information flow, and cross-border interdependence accelerate?

## Q-007 — Trust and Cooperation

What conditions allow large groups of strangers, organizations, and states to sustain trust and cooperation over long periods?

## Q-008 — Wealth, Inequality, and Opportunity

How can a society preserve incentives and dynamism while preventing extreme concentration of power and opportunity?

## Q-009 — AI-majority Society

How should governance, rights, economic participation, and accountability evolve if artificial agents become a large share of economically or cognitively active actors?

Candidate experiment:

```text
EXP-CIV-005 / AI Majority
```

## Q-010 — Planetary Coordination

Under what conditions can humanity coordinate at planetary scale while preserving meaningful local autonomy and diversity?

Candidate experiment:

```text
EXP-CIV-006 / Earth Council
```

---

# 3. Health, Life, and Biological Evolution

## Q-011 — Future Pandemic Resilience

If humanity again faces a pandemic comparable to the great plagues of history, how can detection, coordination, treatment, logistics, communication, and social resilience improve?

## Q-012 — Healthy Lifespan Extension

What happens to family, education, work, wealth, identity, and politics if healthy human lifespan expands dramatically?

Candidate experiment:

```text
EXP-CIV-004 / The Long-Life Society
```

## Q-013 — Genetic Evolution and Intervention

How should humanity model, understand, and govern natural genetic evolution, gene editing, reproductive technology, and heritable intervention?

## Q-014 — Human Biological Adaptation beyond Earth

What biological adaptations, medical systems, or engineered interventions may be needed for long-duration life in low gravity, radiation, closed habitats, and other planetary environments?

---

# 4. Climate, Ecology, and Planetary Resilience

## Q-015 — Climate Adaptation

How can societies adapt to long-term climate change while protecting food, water, infrastructure, health, migration stability, and ecosystems?

## Q-016 — Extreme Water Management

If humanity again faces problems comparable in scale to the great historical flood-control challenges, how can sensing, forecasting, infrastructure, governance, ecological design, and AI improve the response?

## Q-017 — Ecological Recovery

How can severely degraded ecosystems recover without sacrificing basic human development and economic resilience?

## Q-018 — Civilization after Environmental Collapse

Can a civilization rebuild after catastrophic climate and infrastructure collapse without reproducing the same structural weaknesses?

Candidate experiment:

```text
EXP-CIV-002 / After the Flood
```

---

# 5. Energy, Materials, and Industrial Capability

## Q-019 — Abundant Clean Energy

What technical, economic, and institutional conditions are required for civilization to move toward abundant, reliable, low-carbon energy?

## Q-020 — Materials Discovery Acceleration

How can data, simulation, AI, automated experimentation, and manufacturing feedback shorten the path from material hypothesis to useful industrial material?

## Q-021 — Closed-loop Industrial Systems

How can industrial systems reduce dependence on continuous virgin-resource extraction through recycling, repair, circular production, and autonomous resource management?

## Q-022 — Extreme-environment Materials

What materials are required for deep ocean, high radiation, extreme temperature, vacuum, lunar, Martian, and deep-space environments?

---

# 6. Planetary and Multi-body Civilization

## Q-023 — First Cross-body Civilization System

When human survival and activity expand from Earth into cislunar space, how should humanity build its first cross-body civilization system?

Sub-questions include:

- transport;
- energy;
- communication;
- resources;
- habitation;
- economy;
- governance;
- ecology;
- safety;
- international coordination.

Candidate experiments:

```text
EXP-SPACE-001 / Cislunar Logistics
EXP-ENERGY-001 / Lunar Power Grid
EXP-GOV-001 / Multi-body Governance
```

## Q-024 — Sustainable Mars Civilization

What conditions allow a Mars settlement to survive, adapt, and become a self-sustaining civilization without continuous direct support from Earth?

Canonical experiment:

```text
EXP-CIV-001 / Ares Alpha
```

## Q-025 — Interplanetary Governance

How should law, sovereignty, identity, trade, rights, and conflict resolution work when communities are separated by planets and communication delay?

## Q-026 — Space Resource Economy

How could lunar, asteroid, orbital, and planetary resources become part of a stable economy without recreating destructive extraction and geopolitical conflict?

---

# 7. Long-term Civilization and Cosmic Expansion

## Q-027 — Civilization Resilience and the Great Filter

What forms of technological, institutional, ethical, and cultural development reduce the probability that a civilization destroys itself as its power increases?

## Q-028 — Civilization Quality

How should civilization quality be measured beyond GDP, military power, or raw technological capability?

Candidate dimensions:

- survival resilience;
- adaptability;
- knowledge preservation;
- cooperation;
- innovation;
- ecological sustainability;
- institutional learning;
- individual flourishing;
- long-term option value.

## Q-029 — Multi-planet Civilization

What is the minimum viable architecture for a civilization that persists across multiple planets or habitats?

## Q-030 — Interstellar Civilization

What technical, social, cultural, biological, and governance systems are required before humanity can become a durable interstellar civilization?

## Question-to-Experiment Mapping

A question may generate multiple experiments.

Example:

```text
Q-023
First Cross-body Civilization System

├── EXP-SPACE-001  Cislunar Logistics
├── EXP-ENERGY-001 Lunar Power Grid
├── EXP-CIV-007    Cislunar Civilization
└── EXP-GOV-001    Multi-body Governance
```

Another example:

```text
Q-024
Sustainable Mars Civilization

└── EXP-CIV-001 Ares Alpha
```

This mapping is intentionally many-to-many.

One experiment may also test several questions at once.

## Research Lifecycle

Each research question should move through the following stages when appropriate:

```text
Q-xxx
Question registered

↓

Research framing
Variables, evidence, mechanisms, uncertainty

↓

Model / Scenario / Experiment

↓

Observation and comparison

↓

Insight

↓

Decision or action implication

↓

Reality feedback

↓

Question refinement
```

The agenda is therefore intended to evolve continuously.

---

## Operating Principle

X-LAB should not attempt to answer every question at once.

The agenda exists to preserve the important questions, prioritize them, connect them to experiments, and accumulate knowledge over time.

> The research agenda asks what humanity should understand before reality makes the experiment irreversible.
